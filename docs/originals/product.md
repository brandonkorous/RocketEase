# Product

## Definition

RocketEase is the operating system for social marketing. It gives businesses and agencies one workspace to plan content, publish across networks, handle conversations, coordinate approvals, manage paid promotion, and understand organic and paid outcomes together.

**Positioning:** Effortless Launch. Better by Design.

The category is larger than social scheduling. The differentiating promise is a connected operating model: content can become a post, a winning post can become an ad, responses retain customer context, and performance returns to planning.

## Product principles

1. One source of truth. Campaigns, content, conversations, spend, and results share identifiers and history.
2. Native where it matters. Common work is normalized, while platform-specific controls remain available and clearly labeled.
3. Safe publishing. Preview, validation, approval, idempotency, audit history, and recoverable failures are defaults.
4. Organic and paid belong together. Users can compare and promote without reconstructing a campaign in another system.
5. Collaboration is in the work. Comments, decisions, assignees, versions, and deadlines attach to the affected object.
6. Complexity appears progressively. New users see the next useful action; experts retain dense controls and shortcuts.
7. Neutral product identity. RocketEase supplies black, white, and structure; platform color appears only for platform identity or data.

## Target outcomes

- Reduce time from idea to approved, scheduled content.
- Increase the percentage of messages handled within team targets.
- Make cross-channel and paid/organic performance understandable without spreadsheet stitching.
- Let agencies operate many client workspaces without accidental cross-client access or publishing.
- Turn successful content into reusable assets, templates, and promotion candidates.

## Primary capability areas

- Dashboard and attention queue
- Planner and calendar
- Composer, previews, publishing, and scheduling
- Unified inbox and customer context
- Campaigns and ads
- Analytics, reports, exports, and comparisons
- Content library and reusable templates
- Approvals, comments, assignments, and version history
- Connected accounts and integration health
- Organizations, workspaces, teams, billing, security, and notifications
- Purpose-built mobile dashboard, inbox, calendar, and composer

## MVP boundary

MVP proves the connected workflow for Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, and TikTok where provider APIs permit it. It includes workspace setup, account connection, calendar, composer, scheduling, publishing status, basic inbox, approvals, content library, campaign grouping, unified core analytics, CSV export, audit history, and responsive/mobile-critical flows.

MVP may link to native ad managers for unsupported creation controls while importing campaign performance. Direct ad creation, advanced attribution, automation, CRM enrichment, white-label client portals, and additional networks follow behind capability and demand gates.

## Non-goals for initial release

- Replacing a general CRM, DAM, project-management suite, or full ad-buying terminal
- Promising feature parity across providers
- Autonomous publishing or spend changes without explicit policy and human control
- Invented benchmarks, recommendations, or cross-network metrics that hide definition differences

## Business model assumptions

Subscription tiers should scale on workspaces, users, connected channels, reporting depth, approval controls, and support—not opaque usage traps. Provider pass-through costs and high-volume ingestion may require fair-use limits. Final packaging requires market and cost validation.

## Success measures

- Activation: workspace created, one channel connected, and first post scheduled or first conversation handled within seven days.
- Core value: weekly active workspaces completing at least two lifecycle actions.
- Reliability: publish success and webhook/sync freshness meet targets in `requirements.md`.
- Collaboration: approval turnaround time and overdue work trend downward.
- Retention: activated workspace retention by persona and plan.
- Trust: low unauthorized-action, duplicate-publish, and cross-workspace exposure incident rates.
