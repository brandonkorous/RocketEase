# Users

## Primary audiences

### Small-business owner or solo marketer

Owns strategy and execution. Needs a quick path from idea to scheduled post, a single inbox, understandable results, and confidence that nothing failed. Low tolerance for setup complexity.

### In-house social media manager

Runs a high-volume calendar across several channels. Needs channel-specific variants, reliable scheduling, asset reuse, response queues, reporting, and operational visibility.

### Marketing lead

Sets goals and approves work. Needs campaign-level views, paid/organic comparison, budget and outcome visibility, approval controls, and trustworthy reports without daily production detail.

### Agency operator

Switches among client workspaces. Needs strict separation, cross-client attention summaries, templated workflows, client approvals, branded reporting, and safeguards against publishing to the wrong account.

### Creator, designer, or copywriter

Contributes assets and drafts. Needs briefs, comments, version history, due dates, reusable content, and a narrow view of assigned work without access to billing or unrelated clients.

### Community or support manager

Handles comments, mentions, reviews, and messages. Needs prioritized queues, assignment, saved replies, customer context, internal notes, response-time targets, and clear platform origin.

### Analyst or executive viewer

Consumes performance rather than producing work. Needs consistent metric definitions, comparisons, campaign attribution, exports, scheduled reports, and read-only access.

### Workspace administrator

Manages members, roles, connections, security, billing, notifications, and audit records. Needs explicit impact previews and recoverable configuration.

## Jobs to be done

- When planning a campaign, coordinate content, owners, approvals, channels, spend, and targets in one place.
- When adapting content across networks, preserve the idea while satisfying each platform’s formats and limits.
- When a customer responds, see context and ownership so the team answers once, correctly, and quickly.
- When content succeeds, promote or reuse it without losing the connection to its original performance.
- When reporting, explain what happened across paid and organic channels with honest metric definitions.
- When operating many clients, see urgent work globally while preventing cross-client mistakes.

## Access and context assumptions

A person may belong to several organizations and workspaces with different roles in each. Role and workspace are always visible in consequential flows. Agency users need a global client overview, but data mutation occurs inside an explicit workspace context.

## Accessibility and inclusivity

Support keyboard-only and assistive-technology users, international names, multiple locales and timezones, and users with limited platform expertise. Avoid relying on slang, platform color, precision pointing, or perfect vision. Localization architecture begins at MVP even if the first release is English-only.

## Anti-personas

Initial releases are not built for political persuasion operations, unbounded automated engagement, anonymous bulk account farms, or teams requiring a full enterprise CRM/ad-trading replacement. Policy and abuse controls must prevent high-risk automation patterns.
