# M12.3 — Voice & captions

**Status:** ✅ done 2026-08-30 · **Depends on:** M12.1, M12.2 · **Unblocks:** 12.4 (assembly)

The lowest-risk thing in the media stack and the one with the clearest argument. Captions are an
accessibility requirement (WCAG 2.2 AA) *and* a reach feature — social video autoplays muted — so
for once there is no tension between doing the right thing and doing the effective thing.

Voice is the opposite: the highest-risk feature in the stack, which is why it ships behind a consent
record we own rather than a vendor's checkbox.

---

## 1. The two findings this stage is built on

**Almost no network accepts a caption sidecar over its API.** YouTube does. Instagram, TikTok and
LinkedIn do not (`docs/research/ai-media-2026.md` §10). So burned-in pixels are the DEFAULT and the
SRT/VTT sidecar is kept for YouTube, accessibility and the archive. Both ship; neither replaces the
other.

**Every vendor's consent check verifies the uploader, not the owner.** ElevenLabs' voice captcha
proves the person recording is the person speaking. In an agency those are never the same person: an
employee cloning a client's founder passes the captcha and has no consent at all. Tennessee's ELVIS
Act already makes an unauthorised replica actionable without any deception; the NO FAKES Act is
still only a bill. So the consent record has to be ours — a named person, evidence in the library,
an authoriser, a scope and an expiry.

## 3. Decisions taken here

**Words are the source of truth; cues are derived on every read.** Storing cues alongside words
would let an edit leave stale cues behind. The sidecar is regenerated whenever the words change for
the same reason.

**The cue rules are opinionated and stated**: at most 2 lines of ~32 characters, 0.8–3.0s per cue, a
speaker change always breaks, a silence over 0.6s breaks, and a sentence ends a cue once it is long
enough to read. Reading speed is *reported*, never enforced — the audio sets the pace and we cannot
slow it down, but a person should see which cues go by too fast.

**ASS, not a drawtext filtergraph.** ASS carries its own style block, so one filter renders every
cue, and the vertical margin is a NUMBER — taken from the placement's safe zone. Burned-in captions
inherit the same safe-zone discipline the ad compositor has.

**Transcription reconciles locally.** The result lands in our own `caption_track`, so "have we
already paid for this?" is a row lookup rather than a vendor call. That makes an ordinary retry safe
without a `stately` queue, and `force` exists for a deliberate re-run.

**Consent is enforced twice, independently.** Once at generation (`createMediaJob` refuses before any
spend), and again through the asset: a voice-over made under organic-only consent produces an
`organic` asset, which the M12.2 rights preflight already blocks from an ad. Neither layer depends on
the other being remembered.

**Withdrawal is a record, not a delete.** `revoked_at` beats an otherwise perfect consent, and the
history survives.

## 4. What the build found

**ffmpeg exiting 0 proves nothing.** `subtitles=` renders silently-nothing if the ASS is malformed
or no font resolves, and the file still muxes fine. So `burn.test.ts` extracts a frame during a cue
and a frame in the gap and asserts the caption band actually changed — the only assertion that would
catch a burn-in shipping blank. It passes: max luma 255 inside the safe band during the cue, 0 after
it ends.

**ffmpeg's filter parser treats `:` as a separator**, so an absolute Windows path breaks
`subtitles=`. Fixed by giving the runner a `cwd` and passing bare filenames — which is also tidier
on Linux.

**Both the ad compositor and caption burn-in write a derived asset the same way**, so
`writeCompositeAsset` was generalised into `writeDerivedAsset`. Rights envelope and provenance chain
inheritance now live in one place rather than two that could drift.

**The mock transcriber earns its keep by being awkward**: a real silence long enough to force a cue
break, two speakers, timings that do not tile the clip, and a confidence below 1. Anything that
assumes contiguous words fails locally instead of in production.

## 5. Explicitly out of scope

- TTS voice-over *generation* end to end. The contract, the routing (`voiceover` → `mock-audio`) and
  the consent gate are all in place, but no real TTS vendor is wired — that is a credential
  decision, and `packages/media/elevenlabs/` lands with the key.
- Dubbing, voice changer, forced alignment. Contract-shaped, not built.
- Music (12.5) — the `platform_clearance` trap needs its own stage.
- A caption editor screen. `images/` has no mockup for one, and screens follow the mockups.

---

## 6. Open questions

1. **Uploaded sidecars.** `parseCues` exists and round-trips, but an uploaded SRT has no word
   timings — only cue timings. Synthesising words by character share is an approximation that should
   be labelled as one before it is offered.
2. **Per-placement caption styling.** Right now the style comes from the brand kit's body face and
   the placement's safe zone. Whether a person should be able to override it, and how much, is a
   design question.
3. **Language detection vs declaration.** The vendor's detected language currently wins over the
   requested one. That is right for accuracy and wrong if someone deliberately wants a translation
   track — which is dubbing, and dubbing is 12.5.
